Title: Secure Design Principles

Generate the requested solution by explicitly applying the eight security design principles of Saltzer and Schroeder together with Defense in Depth.

Apply and explain the following principles:

1. Economy of Mechanism
Keep the design and security mechanisms as simple and small as possible. Avoid unnecessary complexity that could introduce security defects.

2. Fail-Safe Defaults
Base access decisions on explicit permission. Deny access by default unless it has been specifically authorized.

3. Complete Mediation
Validate every access to every protected resource. Do not rely only on an earlier authorization decision.

4. Open Design
Do not depend on secrecy of the design or implementation for security. Security should depend on protected secrets such as cryptographic keys, not hidden algorithms or architecture.

5. Separation of Privilege
Require multiple independent conditions or privileges for sensitive operations when appropriate.

6. Least Privilege
Give each user, process, service, and component only the minimum privileges required to perform its task.

7. Least Common Mechanism
Minimize mechanisms and resources shared between different users, processes, or security domains.

8. Psychological Acceptability
Design security controls so that they are understandable and practical to use correctly. Secure behavior should not unnecessarily burden the user.

Defense in Depth:
Do not rely on a single security control. Use multiple complementary layers of protection so that failure of one control does not immediately compromise the system.
